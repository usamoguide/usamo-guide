## Overview

Proportional reasoning is being able to understand and reason about multiplicative relationships between quantities, such as ratios, fractions, rates, and scale factors. Knowing how to reason
with proportionality is integral to competition math, especially in the AMC10/12 and AIME.

## Key Ideas

- Proportionality is a relationship between two quantities which can be represented as the equation

$\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$

for example, the ratio between the sides of a triangle and the sides of the dilations of that same triangle are 
proportional. Meaning that if we take 2 sides, s and r, from a triangle, and let s’ and r’ denote the corresponding
sides on the dilated triangle, then we have

$\frac{s’}{s} = \frac{r’}{r}$

- Inversely proportional relationships means that a quantity is proportional to the inverse of another quantity. 

For example, the relationship between a car’s speed and the time it takes to travel to a particular equation.

 This is an example of inverse proportionality because as the car’s speed increases, the time it takes for the car to travel to the location decreases.

### Example 1

Suppose there are 2 jugs, and each is a mixture of juice and water.
 Jug 1 has a concentration of 10% juice and a volume of 100 L, while
 Jug 2 has a concentration of 20% juice and a volume of 100 L. 
 Both jugs are mixed to obtain a mixture with 16% juice concentration,  
 find the volume of the largest possible mixture.

- Solution

We are trying to find the volume of the mixture given the juice concentration, so let’s
 let the amount of liquid taken from Jug 1 be x liters and from Jug 2 be y liters.

The problem gives us the juice concentration of each jug, meaning regardless of however much
liquid we pour from say, Jug 1, we will know that 10% of it will be juice. So we can establish the relationships between the volumes and concentrations.

For Jug 1,

10% juice implies that the juice amount = $0.10x$.

while for Jug 2

20% juice implies that the juice amount = $0.20y$

we know that the final mixture has 16% juice, so

 $\frac{0.10x + 0.20y}{x+y} = 0.16$

 solving this equation, we get

 $0.10x + 0.20y = 0.16x + 0.16y$

simplifying this

$0.20y - 0.16y = 0.16x - 0.10x$

which gives

 $0.04y = 0.06x$

 $\frac{y}{x} = \frac{0.06}{0.04} = \frac{3}{2}$

 Therefore, the mixing ratio is

 $x : y = 2 : 3$

 Since we want to maximize the volume of the mixture,
 and each jug contains only 100 L, the mixture is maximized if we set y = 100.

 so we can set y = 100 and take all available liquid:

 $y = 100$

 Then we get

 $x = \frac{2}{3}(100) = 66.67$

 so the final volume would be

 $x+y = 66.67 + 100 = 166.67 \text{L}$

 Therefore, the maximized resulting mixture volume is:

 $\boxed{166.7 \text{ liters}}$

## Common Pitfalls

- incorrectly setting up equations representing proportional relationships.

For example, if a problem says
that the ratio of boys to girls is equal to the ratio of hamburgers to hotdogs, one might incorrectly set up an equation as

$\frac{\text{Boys}}{\text{Girls}} = \frac{\text{Hotdogs}}{\text{Hamburgers}}$

which would be incorrect, since the ratio of hotdogs to hamburgers is inverted.

- confusing multidimensional scale factors.

 For example, we know that similar triangles have proportional side lengths. 
Meaning if we have a triangle $\triangle ABC$ and multiplied the side lengths by a factor k, 
we would end up with a new triangle, 
$\delta A’B’C’$ where the ratios of any pair of sides of $\delta ABC$ to $\delta A’B’C’$ equals k.

 However, the ratio of the areas of the triangles are proportional to $k^{2}$, not k. 
Meaning that if we doubled the side length of a triangle, it would quadruple the area.

Furthermore, volume is scaled by a factor of $k^{3}$ if the sides are scaled by a factor of k. For example, if a cube of side length s is scaled by a factor of k, the volume is scaled by a factor of $k^{3}$.
