## Overview

Right triangles are triangles which contain one right angle. Right triangles will only ever have 1 right angle because the sum of the angles
in a triangle will always add up to 180 degrees.

Right angle triangles are very common in AMC10/12 and early AIME problems. However, there are 2 unique right triangles
which have special rules which can help you cut time and reduce confusion. 

These 2 triangles are the 30-60-90 triangle, and the 45-45-90 triangle.

## Key Ideas

### 30-60-90 Triangles

30-60-90 triangles are triangles with the angles 30°, 60°, and 90°. (or $\frac{\pi}{6}$, $\frac{\pi}{3}$, and $\frac{\pi}{2}$, respectively).

 30-60-90 triangles have an interesting property. Let $\Delta ABC$ be a 30-60-90 triangle 
with $\angle A = 30^\circ$, $\angle B = 60^\circ$, and $\angle C = 90^\circ$, 
and let a,b, and c be the side lengths corresponding to each angle, respectively. Then by the Law of Sines, we have

$\frac{b}{\sin60^{\circ}} = \frac{a}{\sin30^{\circ}}$

simplifying the fractions, we get 

$b = \sqrt{3} a$

now, we can solve for c in terms of a

$\frac{\sqrt{3} a}{\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}} = \frac{c}{1}$

which gives 

$c = 2a$

This means, that the ratio of the sides $a:b:c$, is always equal to $1:\sqrt{3}:2$. 

Or in other words,
if x is the length of the side opposite of the 30 degree angle, then the length of the side opposite of the
60 degree angle is $\sqrt{3} x$. Additionally, the length of the hypotenuse is exactly 2x.

This special property of the 30-60-90 right triangle can be used to find the sides of a triangle given only 1 side.

### Example 1

Given triangle $\triangle ABC$, with $\angle A = 30^{\circ}$, $\angle B = 60^{\circ}$, and $\angle C = 90^{\circ}$,
and corresponding sides a, b, and c. Suppose a = 12, find c.

Draw a diagram if you need to.

 here we are given the length of the side opposite of the 30° angle, so since the triangle
 is a 30-60-90 triangle, we know that hypotenuse is 2 times the length of the side opposite of the
30° angle. 

We can use the ratio then to form an equation

$c = 2a$

and we get our answer, 24.

### 45-45-90 Triangles

Let $\triangle ABC$ be a triangle with $\angle A = 45^{\circ}$, $\angle B = 90^{\circ}$, and $\angle C = 45^{\circ}$, with side lengths
a, b, and c, respectively.

then, using the Law of Sines, we have

$\frac{a}{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}} = \frac{b}{1}$

simplifying gives

$b = \sqrt{2} a$

additionally, since $\triangle ABC$ is an isosceles triangle, a = c, and the relationship also follows for c.
This means that for a 45-45-90 triangle, the ratio of sides $a : b : c$ will always be $1 : \sqrt{2} : 1$

In other words, if we let x be the lengths of the sides opposite of the 45° angles, 
then the length of the hypotenuse is $\sqrt{2}x$.

This relation is much easier to remember since 45-45-90 triangles are isosceles.

### Example 2

let $\triangle ABC$ be a triangle with angles $\angle A = 45^{\circ}$, $\angle B = 90^{\circ}$, and $\angle C = 45^{\circ}$, and sides
a, b, and c, respectively. If a = $6\sqrt{2}$, 
find b and c.

draw a diagram if needed.

 Firstly, we know that this triangle is isosceles, therefore c is equal to $6 \sqrt{2}$ aswell. 

Next, since we know that the ratio of the leg to the hypotenuse in a 45-45-90 triangle is $1 : \sqrt{2}$,
we can form an equation

$c = \sqrt{2} a$

plugging in a = $6 \sqrt{2}$

we get c = 12.

## Common Pitfalls

- incorrectly setting “x”. 

 For example, if you are dealing with a 30-60-90 triangle 
 where the hypotenuse length is known, 
 then make sure you set the length equal to 2x to find the length of the legs, NOT x.

- applying these rules to triangles not mentioned

not all triangles follow patterns like these, 
make sure that the angles match before you begin applying
the rules you see here.

- forgetting the ratios

self explanatory, forgetting the ratio will result in an incorrect equation and thus a wrong answer.
