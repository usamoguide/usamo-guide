## Welcome to USAMO Guide

Welcome to USAMO Guide, a free olympiad mathematics guide which takes you from
your very first math contest all the way to the International Mathematical
Olympiad and beyond. It's named for the USAMO (the USA Mathematical
Olympiad), and the track names follow the US contests. Olympiad mathematics is
the same all across the world, though, so the guide works for students from
any country. The problems don't care where you live.

## What are tracks?

So what actually is the guide? At the core are tracks. USAMO Guide is split into specific sections; these are what we call tracks. Tracks are sets of modules (each of which consists of a reading and practice problems, see more below) for each specific stage of one's
olympiad mathematics journey. For example, the Intermediate track correlates
with the AMC 10, or with the first stage in other countries, such as the IOQM
in India. Look at the table below to see how each track maps to its US contest
and to its counterpart in other countries.

| Track | US contest | Equivalent stage in other countries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foundations | AMC 8 | Junior or entry-level national rounds |
| Intermediate | AMC 10/12 | First national round (short-answer or multiple choice) |
| Advanced | AIME | Second national round or junior olympiad |
| Olympiad | USAJMO / USAMO | National olympiad, regional olympiads, up to the IMO |

(For the actual contests in each country, and how one leads to the next, see
[Introduction to Olympiad Mathematics](/general/intro-olympiad-math).)

## Where should I start?

The guide is built so that you can start anywhere, from any track. If you're
completely brand new to olympiad mathematics, start with the
**[Foundations](/foundations)** track. If you're comfortable with the basics or
are aiming for the AMC 10 and 12 (and equivalent elsewhere), go with the **[Intermediate](/intermediate)**
track. If you've qualified for the AIME (or the equivalent in your country)
and are studying for that, go with the **[Advanced](/advanced)** track, and
past that, work on the **[Olympiad](/usamo)** track for proof-based
mathematics.

One thing to note is that you don't have to finish one track before moving on
to the next. Each module lists the prerequisites for its topic at the top of
the page, so you can start from wherever you'd like, with any module in any
track.

## How modules are laid out

Modules follow a predictable format: an introduction, the motivation, an
explanation of the topic, worked examples, and finally practice problems
sorted from Very Easy to Insane. Of the practice problems, the starred ones are
those we most recommend solving.

## Tracking your progress

USAMO Guide also has some neat features to help you keep track of your work.
You can set a status on each module (Not Started, Reading, Practicing,
Complete, Skipped, or Ignored) and on each problem (Not Attempted, Solving,
Solved, Reviewing, Skipped, or Ignored). Your **[dashboard](/dashboard)**
shows your progress and activity and links back to the module you were last
on, and the **[Problems page](/problems)** lists problems from every category
and difficulty, which you can search and filter.
