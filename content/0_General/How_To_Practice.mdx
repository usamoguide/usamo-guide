## Practice just above your level

You learn best when you struggle through a concept or a problem. Your brain
crawling through its own internal network, searching for the correct tool,
concept, and its implementation whilst you dwell on the problem, is how you
actually get better.

You want to be at a level where you're not able to solve problems easily, but
where they're not too difficult either. If you're able to solve all the
problems very easily, move up a level. If you rarely solve any, move down one.
Aim for about half of the problems you attempt being solved after real effort.

## Upsolve every contest

On top of this, I recommend upsolving. After every contest or mock test you
do, reattempt the problems you missed (with a small hint if needed) before
reading the solutions. These problems are generally the most important ones,
because they're right at the edge of your ability.

## Take timed mock tests

Aside from simply practicing topic problems, timed mock tests are very
important as well. Topic practice only teaches you the actual mathematics.
Meanwhile, full timed mock tests teach you how to actually take the exam:
pacing, when to skip a problem, and so on. Do at least a few mock tests in the
month before the contest, with a real time limit, and no calculator or notes
either. For the AMC, past papers are on the
[AoPS Wiki](https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/AMC_Problems_and_Solutions),
while other contests usually have theirs on their own websites.

## Keep an error log

For problems in general, it's important to keep an error log. Write down the
problem, what went wrong, and the idea you missed, then reread the log before
contests. Mistakes repeat (annoyingly often), so once you spot the pattern, you
can fix the issue fairly easily.

## Learn from solutions

Try to predict each step of a solution before you read it, and only open the
solution after a real attempt: 20 to 30 minutes for a short-answer problem, and
much longer for an olympiad problem (there's more on this in
[Getting Unstuck](/general/getting-unstuck)). Even if you're hopelessly stuck,
I still wouldn't open the whole solution. Look at just the first step, or
maybe a small hint, and reattempt the problem from there.

When you do read a solution, go one step at a time and try to fully digest
each step. Afterwards, I'd recommend writing down the key idea of the solution
in one sentence, along with what should have pointed you to it. Then, a few
days later, once it's noted in your error log, try to solve the problem again
from scratch.

## Review over time

Review is also important, since you forget techniques you don't use. Every few
weeks, redo old starred problems and go back over the problems in your error
log. USAMO Guide has a handy little feature for this too: you can mark
problems as "Reviewing," and they'll appear as such, on your dashboard and elsewhere.

## Quality over quantity

Fifty problems you thought hard about and struggled through will teach you far
more than two hundred you skimmed. If you weren't able to do a problem,
especially try and write out the full solution, because that's when you find the steps you'd skipped over in your head or had done
incorrectly.
