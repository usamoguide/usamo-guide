## What is olympiad mathematics?

Simply put, in school mathematics you're taught to apply specific methods to
specific problems. In olympiad mathematics, however, you have to find the
method yourself, on top of figuring out its implementation. Expect a few basic
topics: algebra, geometry, number theory, and combinatorics. Many
problems mix and match several of these topics (and subtopics/concepts) in the same problem, so you have to
search through a whole variety of tools, formulae, and such to solve them.

There are generally two types of olympiad contests/rounds. There are
short-answer or multiple-choice rounds, where you're either given a few
options to choose from or simply have to write an integer answer. Here is
where speed and accuracy matter most. There are also proof-based rounds,
where you're given a few hard problems over a few hours and have to write down
a full argument, a full proof, for each one. Don't expect to encounter
proof-based rounds or contests until a high level, though.

It takes multiple years to reach a national olympiad. Starting later than your
friends is fine, because practicing regularly is what matters most, not when
you started.

## The International Mathematical Olympiad

The **International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO)** is *the* high school
olympiad, held every July, and each country can send a team of up to six
students. Making that team is hard. The usual route is a wide first round, a
narrower second round, a proof-based national olympiad, and then a training
camp with team selection tests on top of all that. In the US, for example,
that's the AMC, then the AIME, then the USAMO, and finally MOP.

## The US pathway

In the US, most people start with the AMC 8 in middle school, although if
you're already in high school you can go straight to the AMC 10 or 12.

| Contest | Format | Who takes it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **AMC 8** | 25 multiple-choice problems | Middle schoolers. It doesn't qualify you for anything. |
| **AMC 10** / **AMC 12** | 25 multiple-choice problems, held in November on two dates (A and B) | Grade 10 and below / grade 12 and below |
| **AIME** | 15 short-answer problems | Qualifiers from the AMC 10 or 12 |
| **USAJMO** / **USAMO** | Two days of proof-based problems | AIME qualifiers from the AMC 10 / AMC 12 |
| **MOP** (Mathematical Olympiad Program) | Summer camp, followed by selection tests for the six-person US IMO team | Top USA(J)MO scorers |

In the United States, USAMO qualification is based only on AIME scores, and
the AIME cutoffs are 100 on the AMC 10 and 85 on the AMC 12 (all of which are
subject to change by the MAA).

### How to register for the AMC

So how do you actually register for the AMC? You can't register individually.
A school or site **competition manager** has to register with the MAA and then
sign you up to take the test. First, ask your school's math department. If they
don't offer the AMC, ask a teacher to become a competition manager, or find
another school, college, or testing site that takes outside students.

Make sure your details and logistics are sorted out a month or two early,
because sites order their exams weeks ahead of time.

Generally, the AMC 8 is in January, the AMC 10/12 in November, the AIME in
February, and the USA(J)MO in March. Exact dates are on the
[MAA website](https://maa.org/student-programs/amc/) (they shift a little
every year).

## Pathways in other countries

Outside the US the names are different, but the idea is the same. For example in India,
it's the IOQM first, then the RMO, then the INMO.

| Country | First round(s) | National olympiad | Organiser |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| United Kingdom | UKMT Mathematical Challenges | BMO Round 1 → Round 2 | [UKMT](https://www.ukmt.org.uk/) |
| Canada | COMC | CMO | [Canadian Mathematical Society](https://cms.math.ca/competitions/) |
| India | IOQM → RMO | INMO | [HBCSE](https://olympiads.hbcse.tifr.res.in/) |
| Australia | Australian Mathematics Competition | AMO | [Australian Maths Trust](https://www.amt.edu.au/) |
| Singapore | SMO (Junior, Senior, Open) | SMO Open Round 2 | [Singapore Mathematical Society](https://sms.math.nus.edu.sg/) |
| China | National High School Math League | China Mathematical Olympiad | Chinese Mathematical Society |

Formats and dates change, so check the organiser's site for your year. If
your country isn't in the table, look it up on the
[IMO country pages](https://www.imo-official.org/countries.aspx). Many of
them link to the national organiser.

### Regional and international olympiads

If you make it far enough in your country, you might also end up at a regional
olympiad. Usually only national team candidates get sent to these, and in
terms of difficulty they're generally harder than a national olympiad but
easier than the IMO itself.

| Olympiad | Who takes it |
| --- | --- |
| APMO (Asian Pacific Mathematics Olympiad) | Countries around the Pacific Rim |
| EGMO (European Girls' Mathematical Olympiad) | Girls' teams from Europe and, these days, outside it too |
| Balkan MO | Countries in and around the Balkans |
| Iberoamerican Olympiad | Spanish- and Portuguese-speaking countries |
| RMM (Romanian Master of Mathematics) | A small invitation-only contest, with some of the hardest problems outside the IMO |

Past papers for all of these are online, and even if you never sit any of
them, they make for great olympiad practice.
