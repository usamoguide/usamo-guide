## Being stuck is normal

Everyone gets stuck, and they get stuck all the time. The difference is that
strong problem solvers know to fall back on a few go-to things, a few tools and
concepts they can apply when their brain runs dry. The next time you have no
idea where to begin a problem, or no idea how to continue one you've started,
work through the list below.

## Things to try

Every single thing on this list is supposed to give you more intuition about
the problem, even if it doesn't solve it outright.

1. Reread the problem and underline every condition. If you don't know what's
   being asked for, you're obviously going to have a very hard time with the
   problem.
2. Test small and extreme cases, like $n = 1, 2, 3, 4$. You're looking for a
   pattern, and even if you don't find one, you'll gain some intuition for the
   whole problem.
3. In geometry problems, it generally helps to draw a figure to scale if one
   isn't already given. This helps you gain intuition about the problem, as
   well as see it from a new perspective. You can also pull a neat little
   trick where you measure lengths and angles on your accurate diagram and
   work from those (often called "rulerbashing").
4. Name the unknown that you're solving for, ask what would make it easier to
   solve for, and work backwards from there.
5. Sometimes, when you're stuck on a problem, it helps to solve a simpler
   version of it first. Again, all of this helps you gain intuition for the
   actual, more complex problem, and you can often apply the methods you used
   on the simpler problem to the full one as well.
6. Organized, systematic casework also helps in many problems. For some
   problems, you might want to split the cases into "good" and "bad" ones and
   figure out the conditions that make each one true. (Sometimes if the good cases are
   messy to count directly, you can also count the bad ones and subtract them from the
   total instead.)
7. Look at the largest, smallest, first, or last object.
8. In processes and games, look for a quantity that never changes or only
   ever goes up.

## How long to stay stuck

If you're stuck on a problem, give it maybe 20 to 30 minutes before you look at
a hint. Once you've got that hint, try to solve the problem again from there.
On very hard problems, take about 45 minutes before you look at the full
solution.

High-level olympiad problems are drastically unlike the rest; a whole
different beast, if you will. Don't feel bothered if it takes you hours or
even days to solve one, because they generally require some sort of key
insight (or several), which can be quite hard to realize at times.

In general, read solutions one step at a time, and stop reading as soon as you
see the next step, so that you can try the rest yourself.

## Asking for help

You have the [AoPS forums](https://artofproblemsolving.com/community) or our
own [USAMO Guide Discord](https://discord.gg/Rb65gHqX8J) for help as well. If
you do end up asking for help online, include the full problem statement and
where it's from. Mention what you've tried so far and where you got stuck as
well, along with whether you're after a hint or the whole solution, so people
know where to start.
