## Community

For communities, I'd recommend the AoPS forums and our Discord. Doing olympiad
mathematics alone can be quite difficult, and having people to talk to about
problems makes it much easier.

<Resources>
  <Resource
    title="USAMO Guide Discord"
    url="https://discord.gg/Rb65gHqX8J"
    starred
  >
    Ask about problems or modules, talk about contests, and hear about guide
    updates before anyone else.
  </Resource>
  <Resource
    source="AoPS"
    title="AoPS Community Forums"
    url="https://artofproblemsolving.com/community"
  >
    Almost every past contest problem, from the AMC up to national olympiads
    around the world, has its own thread here with solutions.
  </Resource>
</Resources>

## What to use at each level

Here are some other resources, grouped by track (or level). Try to stick to
the level you're at right now, since a book a level up from where you are will
be hard to learn from too early.

### Foundations (AMC 8 or an entry national round)

<Resources>
  <Resource
    source="AoPS"
    title="Alcumus"
    url="https://artofproblemsolving.com/alcumus"
    starred
  >
    Free problems that get harder or easier depending on how you're doing. It
    tracks every topic separately, which is nice if you only want to work on,
    say, geometry.
  </Resource>
  <Resource
    source="OL"
    title="Mastering AMC 8"
    url="https://www.omegalearn.org/books"
  >
    Omega Learn's free AMC 8 book. If a topic shows up on the AMC 8, it's
    probably in here.
  </Resource>
  <Resource
    source="AoPS"
    title="Prealgebra and the Introduction series"
    url="https://artofproblemsolving.com/store"
  >
    AoPS throws a problem at you before teaching anything, which is annoying
    at first but really does make things stick. Brand new to contest math?
    Start with Prealgebra.
  </Resource>
</Resources>

### Intermediate (AMC 10/12 or a first national round)

<Resources>
  <Resource
    source="AoPS"
    title="Volume 1: the Basics, Introduction to Algebra, Counting & Probability, Number Theory, Geometry"
    url="https://artofproblemsolving.com/store"
    starred
  >
    If you ask people who made the AIME what they studied from, a lot of them
    will name these.
  </Resource>
  <Resource
    source="OL"
    title="Mastering AMC 10/12"
    url="https://www.omegalearn.org/books"
  >
    The AMC 10/12 version of the Omega Learn book. It's also free, and each
    chapter has a video lecture to go with it.
  </Resource>
  <Resource
    title="LIVE by Po-Shen Loh: Past Contests"
    url="https://live.poshenloh.com/past-contests"
  >
    Po-Shen Loh's website, with past AMC tests and more. It has a good problem
    framework where you can get hints, too.
  </Resource>
  <Resource
    source="AoPSWiki"
    title="Past AMC Problems and Solutions"
    url="https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/AMC_Problems_and_Solutions"
  >
    All the old AMC papers, plus whatever solutions people have posted (often
    several per problem). These are what I'd use for timed mock tests.
  </Resource>
</Resources>

### Advanced (AIME or a second round)

<Resources>
  <Resource
    source="AoPS"
    title="Volume 2: and Beyond, Intermediate Algebra, Intermediate Counting & Probability, Precalculus"
    url="https://artofproblemsolving.com/store"
    starred
  >
    Volume 2 of the AoPS series. These are a lot harder than Volume 1, so save
    them for when AIME problems are your main focus.
  </Resource>
  <Resource
    source="DA"
    title="David Altizio's Handouts"
    url="https://davidaltizio.web.illinois.edu/mathlinks.html"
  >
    David Altizio's handouts on AIME topics. Pick whichever topic you're
    weakest in and work through the problems at the end.
  </Resource>
</Resources>

### Olympiad (national olympiads and the IMO)

<Resources>
  <Resource
    source="EC"
    title="Euclidean Geometry in Mathematical Olympiads"
    url="https://web.evanchen.cc/geombook.html"
    starred
  >
    Evan Chen's book, usually just called EGMO. Most olympiad students will
    recommend it for geometry.
  </Resource>
  <Resource
    title="Problem-Solving Strategies (Arthur Engel)"
    url="https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/b97682"
  >
    Each chapter of Engel's book covers a single technique (invariants,
    induction, the extremal principle, and so on) followed by a long list of
    problems. It's quite dense, so I'd dip into the chapters you need rather
    than read it cover to cover.
  </Resource>
  <Resource
    title="The Art and Craft of Problem Solving (Paul Zeitz)"
    url="https://www.wiley.com/en-us/The+Art+and+Craft+of+Problem+Solving%2C+3rd+Edition-p-9781119239901"
  >
    Zeitz talks through how he'd attack a problem, not just the answer, so
    it's gentler than Engel if proofs are still new to you.
  </Resource>
  <Resource
    title="IMO Problem Archive"
    url="https://www.imo-official.org/problems.aspx"
  >
    Every IMO paper, plus the shortlists. I'd actually spend more time on the
    shortlists, since each year has around thirty problems instead of six.
  </Resource>
</Resources>

## Full catalog

### Combinatorics

<Resources>
  <Resource
    source="AoPSOnline"
    title="AoPS Intermediate Counting Course"
    url="https://artofproblemsolving.com/school/course/intermediatecounting"
  >
    A paid online class on counting: permutations, combinations, bijections,
    and so on, with a lot of problems.
  </Resource>
  <Resource
    source="YZ"
    title="Yufei Zhao: Enumerative Combinatorics Notes"
    url="https://yufeizhao.com/olympiad/"
  >
    Yufei Zhao's notes, with combinatorics examples worked out step by step.
  </Resource>
  <Resource
    source="Brilliant"
    title="Combinatorics Wiki"
    url="https://brilliant.org/wiki/combinatorics/"
  >
    Handy when you've forgotten how some counting trick works and just need a
    quick reminder.
  </Resource>
</Resources>

### Algebra and Functional Equations

<Resources>
  <Resource
    source="AoPSOnline"
    title="AoPS Intermediate Algebra Course"
    url="https://artofproblemsolving.com/school/course/intermediatealgebra"
  >
    A paid class on the algebra manipulation you'll be doing in nearly every
    contest.
  </Resource>
  <Resource
    title="Cut-the-Knot Algebra Collection"
    url="https://www.cut-the-knot.org/algebra.shtml"
  >
    It looks old, but there's a huge amount on there: algebra tricks,
    identities, and write-ups of specific problems.
  </Resource>
  <Resource
    title="IMOmath: Functional Equations"
    url="https://www.imomath.com/index.cgi?page=functionalEquations"
  >
    A collection of olympiad functional equations problems.
  </Resource>
</Resources>

### Geometry

<Resources>
  <Resource
    source="EC"
    title="Euclidean Geometry in Mathematical Olympiads"
    url="https://web.evanchen.cc/geombook.html"
  >
    The same book as above. It starts from angle chasing and builds up to
    projective geometry and complex numbers.
  </Resource>
  <Resource
    source="AoPSOnline"
    title="AoPS Intro/Intermediate Geometry"
    url="https://artofproblemsolving.com/school/course/introductiongeometry"
  >
    AoPS's geometry classes. They start from the basic theorems, but the later
    problems get quite hard.
  </Resource>
  <Resource
    title="Geometry Revisited (Coxeter and Greitzer)"
    url="https://archive.org/details/geometryrevisite0000coxe"
  >
    An old book, but people still use it to learn classical geometry and get
    used to writing proofs.
  </Resource>
</Resources>

### Number Theory

<Resources>
  <Resource
    source="AoPSOnline"
    title="AoPS Number Theory Course"
    url="https://artofproblemsolving.com/school/course/numbertheory"
  >
    AoPS's number theory class, from the basics up to actual contest problems.
  </Resource>
  <Resource
    title="Paul Zeitz: Number Theory Handouts"
    url="https://web.evanchen.cc/handouts/ZeitzNT/ZeitzNT.pdf"
  >
    A long handout of olympiad number theory problems, sorted by which method
    you'd use on them.
  </Resource>
  <Resource
    source="Brilliant"
    title="Number Theory Wiki"
    url="https://brilliant.org/wiki/number-theory/"
  >
    Quick summaries for when you need to look up a number theory fact.
  </Resource>
</Resources>
