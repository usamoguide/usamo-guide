## Why enter more contests?

Your national olympiad pathway might only have two or three contests per year.
Thus, you may want to seek out other contests to gain more practice under time
pressure, exposure to new problem styles, and a chance to meet other students.
On top of this, other contests generally have their own unique team rounds,
which are a different skill entirely and a lot of fun (speaking from
experience).

There are a variety of tournaments. Some are open to students anywhere, while
others are college tournaments run by a university's own students or
mathematical organizations.

## Open to students anywhere

| Contest | Format | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Math Kangaroo](https://www.aksf.org/) | Individual | Held in dozens of countries each spring, for all school ages. |
| [Purple Comet! Math Meet](https://purplecomet.org/) | Team, online | Free, and open to schools worldwide. |
| [Tournament of Towns](https://www.turgor.ru/en/) | Individual | Held in cities around the world, with a junior and a senior level. Close to olympiad style. |
| [USAMO Guide Contests](https://contests.usamoguide.com/) | Individual, online | Our own recurring contests, with rankings. |
| AoPS mock contests | Individual, online | Community-written mock AMCs, AIMEs, and olympiads on the [AoPS forums](https://artofproblemsolving.com/community). |

## US college-run tournaments

Most college tournaments have an individual round and a team round, and many
have a relay or guts round as well. (If you've never done a guts round, it's
essentially a race: problems come out in small sets, and your team works
through them as fast as it can until time runs out.) Some also have online
divisions or allow international teams. Rules differ from competition to
competition, so make sure to check each website before signing up.

| Tournament | Host | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [HMMT](https://www.hmmt.org/) | Harvard and MIT | Two contests a year. HMMT February is much harder than most other tournaments; HMMT November is aimed at newer students. |
| [PUMaC](https://jason.princeton.edu/) | Princeton | Subject tests by division, plus team and power rounds. |
| [SMT](https://www.stanfordmathtournament.com/) | Stanford | Subject tests, team, and guts rounds. |
| [BMT](https://berkeley.mt/) | UC Berkeley | Individual, team, and focus rounds. |
| [CMIMC](https://cmimc.math.cmu.edu/) | Carnegie Mellon | Subject tests, team, and a proof-based power round. |
| [MMATHS](https://mmaths.org/) | Yale | Individual and team rounds. |
| [DMM](https://www.dukemathmeet.org/) (Duke Math Meet) | Duke | Individual, team, relay, and devil rounds. |
| RMT (Rice Math Tournament) | Rice | Subject tests and team rounds. |
| [Caltech Math Meet](https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/Caltech_Math_Meet) (formerly CHMMC) | Caltech | Individual and team rounds. |
| [JHMT](https://www.johnshopkinsmathtournament.com/) | Johns Hopkins | Subject tests and team rounds. |

## Other US contests

| Contest | Format | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [MATHCOUNTS](https://www.mathcounts.org/) | Individual and team | The main US middle school contest, school-based, from chapter rounds up to nationals. |
| [ARML](https://www.arml.com/) | Large teams | High school, run at several sites on the same day. |
| [USAMTS](https://www.usamts.org/) | Individual, proof-based | Free, with a month per round to write up full solutions, which graders comment on. |
| [Math Prize for Girls](https://mathprize.atfoundation.org/) | Individual | Hosted at MIT, by invitation based on AMC scores. |
