## Overview

Vieta's Formulas allow us to find information on the sums and products of the roots of a polynomial without directly solving for them. It relates the coefficients of the polynomial with the symmetric sums of the roots.

## Derivation

Since $r_1, r_2$ are roots of $ax^2 + bx + c$, we can factor:

$$ax^2 + bx + c = a(x - r_1)(x - r_2) = ax^2 - a(r_1+r_2)x + ar_1r_2$$

Matching coefficients gives the quadratic formulas:

$$r_1 + r_2 = -\frac{b}{a}, \qquad r_1 r_2 = \frac{c}{a}$$

The same argument extends to cubics. Since $ax^3 + bx^2 + cx + d = a(x-r_1)(x-r_2)(x-r_3)$, expanding the right side and matching coefficients gives the cubic formulas:

$$r_1+r_2+r_3 = -\frac{b}{a}, \qquad r_1r_2+r_2r_3+r_3r_1 = \frac{c}{a}, \qquad r_1r_2r_3 = -\frac{d}{a}$$

The pattern generalizes to degree $n$: the $k$-th elementary symmetric sum of the roots equals $(-1)^k \dfrac{a_{n-k}}{a_n}$.

## Key Ideas

- $r_1^2 + r_2^2$ is not a symmetric sum directly — rewrite it as $(r_1+r_2)^2 - 2r_1r_2$.
- $\frac{1}{r_1} + \frac{1}{r_2}$ rewrites as $\frac{r_1+r_2}{r_1r_2}$. Always combine fractions first.
- Expanding products like $(r_1+1)(r_2+1)(r_3+1)$ always produces elementary symmetric sums — a very common competition pattern.
- Vieta's works over $\mathbb{C}$: even with complex roots, symmetric expressions are real and computable from the coefficients.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1.** Let $p, q$ be the roots of $t^2 - 7t + 5 = 0$. Find $p^2 + q^2$.

$p+q = 7$, $pq = 5$, so $p^2+q^2 = (p+q)^2 - 2pq = 49 - 10 = \boxed{39}$.

**Example 2.** Let $m, n$ be the roots of $2x^2 + 15x + 16 = 0$. Find $\frac{1}{m} + \frac{1}{n}$.

$m+n = -\frac{15}{2}$, $mn = 8$, so $\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{n} = \frac{m+n}{mn} = \frac{-15/2}{8} = \boxed{-\dfrac{15}{16}}$.

**Example 3.** Let $p, q, r$ be the roots of $t^3 - 2t^2 + 3t - 4 = 0$. Find $(p+1)(q+1)(r+1)$.

$(p+1)(q+1)(r+1) = pqr + (pq+qr+rp) + (p+q+r) + 1 = 4+3+2+1 = \boxed{10}$.

**Example 4.** The roots $r_1, r_2, r_3$ of $x^3 - 2x^2 - 11x + a = 0$ satisfy $r_1 + 2r_2 + 3r_3 = 0$. Find all values of $a$.

Vieta's gives $r_1+r_2+r_3 = 2$. Subtracting: $r_2 + 2r_3 = -2$, so $r_2 = -2-2r_3$ and $r_1 = 4+r_3$. Substituting into $r_1r_2+r_2r_3+r_3r_1 = -11$ yields $3r_3^2+8r_3-3=0$, giving $r_3 = \frac{1}{3}$ or $r_3 = -3$. Thus $a = \boxed{\dfrac{104}{27} \text{ or } 12}$.
