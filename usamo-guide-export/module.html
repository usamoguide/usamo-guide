Kinematics and Rates

Rate problems, proportions and the distance-rate-time formula.

By Macquel Ko and Siddhant Arora·18 min read·11 practice problems

{{ statusLabel }}
On this page
{{ t.label }}

Overview

Warm-up

If 100 machines make 100 donuts in 100 seconds, how long do 5 machines take to make 5 donuts?

Show answerHide answer

100 seconds. Each machine makes one donut every 100 seconds, so 5 machines make 5 donuts in the same 100 seconds.

If you said 5 seconds, you're in good company. Rate problems come up often on the AMC and less often on the AIME. This module covers the distance-rate-time formula and how to combine rates to find an unknown.

Key ideas

Most rate problems come down to one question: how much of the job gets done in one unit of time?

Rule

If someone finishes Y% of a job in Z hours, they finish YZ% of it in one hour, and the whole job in 100ZY hours.

Say Bob takes 5 hours to do 20% of a job. He needs 100 · 520 = 25 hours for all of it, so he does 125 of the job each hour.

d = rt

Here d is distance, r is rate and t is time. The same formula covers anything that builds up at a steady rate, like donuts from a machine or pages read per hour.

Combining rates

When two people work at the same time, add their rates, not their times.

Ana paints a fence in 3 hours and Ben paints it in 6. Together they paint 13 + 16 = 12 of the fence each hour, so the job takes 2 hours.

Worked examples

Example 1·Two trains

Two trains start 300 miles apart and head toward each other, one at 70 miles per hour and the other at 80. How long until they meet?

Show solutionHide solution

The gap closes by 70 + 80 = 150 miles every hour, so the trains meet after 300 ÷ 150 = 2 hours.

Example 2·Filling a pool

Pipe A fills a pool in 6 hours and pipe B fills it in 9. A drain empties the full pool in 18 hours. With both pipes and the drain open, how long does the pool take to fill?

Show solutionHide solution

Each hour the pool gains 16 + 19 − 118 = 29 of its volume, so it fills in 92 = 4.5 hours. The drain counts as a negative rate.
